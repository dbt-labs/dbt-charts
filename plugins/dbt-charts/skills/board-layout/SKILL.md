# Board Layout

`chart` says what YAML is valid; this page says how to arrange a board's elements.

**Sketch the layout before any YAML.** List the questions the board answers, give each an element
(a chart, a KPI, a table or text), and write one line per row, such as `3: chart 1/2 | chart 1/2`.
Add KPIs when a question asks for a headline number, not by habit.

**Size each element by how much it shows; order it by priority or sequence.** These are separate
decisions. A dense answer earns width but may not be critical, so a full-width chart may sit at
the bottom. Otherwise follow the subject's natural sequence, such as a process from prospect to
won.

**A lead reads as the lead.** When one question dominates or unites the board, its chart leads: near
the top, at full width or two thirds beside one supporting chart. Nothing near it may rival its
size: under or beside a two-thirds lead, charts go in thirds or narrower. When no question
dominates, rows split evenly.

**Column edges line up or differ plainly.** Each row's dividing lines, a KPI row's included,
either meet the lines of the row above or sit clearly apart from them. Two thirds over halves, or
over quarters, nearly meets and reads as a mistake.

**Avoid leftover space.** Charts in one row stretch to a shared height; tables, KPIs and text
don't, so beside a taller chart they leave a hole beneath. Stack them until they match its height,
or give them a row of their own. A table takes only the width its columns need.

**An intro is plain text at the top, or there is none:** what the board covers and the period it
shows. Never a tinted block.

**Check the render as a whole.** Does the lead read as the lead? Do the column edges line up? Is
any space left over?
